Process for collecting real operating data

Start with a four-week measurement period. Record source data as it happens, then enter the reconciled totals or averages below.

  1. 1. Fix the period and scope

    Use one calendar month or four complete weeks. Include one production operation and use the same dates for production, sales, payroll, utilities, and overheads.

  2. 2. Weigh every production batch

    For each batch, record feedstock started, sellable output completed, rejects or losses, date, and batch ID. Use calibrated scales and keep kilograms as the common unit.

    Yield = total sellable output kg ÷ total processed feedstock kg

  3. 3. Prove feedstock cost

    Collect supplier invoices and inbound freight. Record kilograms received, invoice value excluding recoverable VAT, freight, and opening/closing stock so only consumed material is charged.

    Cost/kg = (purchases + inbound freight) ÷ kg received

  4. 4. Measure variable conversion costs

    Count packaging issued, log production labor hours, meter equipment electricity where possible, and capture delivery, storage, waste, testing, and consumables. Divide each total by sellable kg.

  5. 5. Reconcile actual sales

    Export completed sales and credit notes. Exclude VAT, cancelled orders, and unpaid sales. Record kg sold, discounts, refunds, payment fees, commissions, and separate by-product revenue.

    Average price/kg = net product revenue ÷ kg sold

  6. 6. Capture fixed and financing costs

    Use the general ledger, bank statement, payroll, rent, insurance, subscriptions, professional fees, loan statement, and asset register. Separate cash overhead, depreciation, interest, and principal repayments.

  7. 7. Reconcile before entering data

    Check opening stock + purchases − closing stock = feedstock consumed. Check output + production loss against input. Check sales totals against payment and accounting reports. Investigate material differences.

  8. 8. Repeat and make the decision

    Run this for at least three representative periods. Use the median as the base case, the weakest period as the downside case, and test whether break-even stays below practical capacity.

Minimum evidence to keep: batch log, scale readings, supplier invoices, packaging counts, time sheets, utility bills or meter readings, sales export, payment-fee report, delivery invoices, payroll, bank/loan statements, and fixed-cost ledger.
Important: Keep “produced” and “sold” quantities separate. This calculator currently models output from utilized production capacity; if inventory changes materially, use only the costs attributable to the period’s sold output or reconcile the inventory movement with your accountant.

Community post ideas

Bilingual soft GRUBZ promotion ideas based on actual Greek Facebook chicken and poultry group research.

The ideas below draw only on the group roster themes and the existing anonymized pattern library from prior accessible research—no member names, post text, or personal details.
  1. 1. Η αλλαγή της εβδομάδας στο κοτέτσι / This week’s coop change
    Ελληνικά: «Κάθε εβδομάδα κάτι μικρό αλλάζει στο κοτέτσι: η σκιά, το νερό, το στεγνό σημείο ή η ώρα της ρουτίνας. Ποια μικρή αλλαγή κάνατε αυτή την εβδομάδα; Στη δική μας ήρεμη ρουτίνα, λίγα GRUBZ μπαίνουν στο τέλος ως λιχουδιά για τις ενήλικες κότες.»
    English: “Every week, something small changes in the coop: shade, water, a dry spot, or the routine timing. What small change did you make this week? In our calm routine, a few GRUBZ come at the end as a treat for adult hens.”
    Suggested format: Four-photo carousel: one practical change per card; GRUBZ appears on the final card.
    Engagement hook: Invite one short “this week I…” comment.
    Why it works: The existing pattern library shows practical care prompts repeatedly create helpful comment threads. The open, low-effort question makes the product a supporting part of a responsible routine.
  2. 2. Το καλάθι πριν από τη φωτογραφία / The basket before the photo
    Ελληνικά: «Πριν τραβήξουμε φωτογραφία το καλάθι με τα αυγά, κάνουμε πάντα έναν ήρεμο έλεγχο στον χώρο. Ποια είναι η δική σας “πριν από το καλάθι” ρουτίνα; Μετά το check, οι ενήλικες κότες μας έχουν λίγο χρόνο για αναζήτηση με GRUBZ.»
    English: “Before we photograph the egg basket, we always do a calm check around the space. What is your own ‘before the basket’ routine? After the check, our adult hens get a little time to forage with GRUBZ.”
    Suggested format: Two-image post: the simple check first, egg basket second.
    Engagement hook: Ask members to finish: “Before collecting, I always…”
    Why it works: Egg-related conversation is a durable high-interest theme in the prior research. A familiar ritual adds practical value and turns an attractive visual into an easy comment prompt.
  3. 3. Η λεπτομέρεια που ξεχωρίζει / The detail that stands out
    Ελληνικά: «Οι ράτσες έχουν τόσες όμορφες λεπτομέρειες—αλλά ποια παρατηρείτε πρώτη στη δική σας κότα; Χρώμα, φτέρωμα, περπάτημα ή χαρακτήρα; Η δική μας αγαπά να ψάχνει λίγα GRUBZ στο γνώριμο σημείο της αυλής.»
    English: “Breeds have so many beautiful details—but which do you notice first in one of your hens: colour, feathers, walk, or personality? Ours loves to search for a few GRUBZ in her familiar corner of the yard.”
    Suggested format: Natural close-up portrait with four on-image answer options.
    Engagement hook: A four-choice comment or story poll.
    Why it works: Breed identity and flock personality were recurring engagement patterns in the accessible historical sample. A choice-based prompt encourages pride and participation without claiming anything about performance.
  4. 4. Το μικρό τεστ αναζήτησης / The tiny forage test
    Ελληνικά: «Μικρό τεστ για ενήλικες κότες: μια μέρα σε μπολ, μια άλλη μέρα με λίγο πασπάλισμα σε καθαρό, κατάλληλο σημείο. Ποιον τρόπο προτιμά η δική σας παρέα; Δείξτε μας “μπολ” ή “αναζήτηση” στα σχόλια.»
    English: “A tiny adult-hen test: one day in a bowl, another day lightly scattered in a clean, suitable spot. Which approach does your flock prefer? Tell us ‘bowl’ or ‘forage’ in the comments.”
    Suggested format: Six-second split-screen reel with a clear Bowl / Forage end card.
    Engagement hook: One-word answer; invite an optional reason.
    Why it works: The prior pattern library combines feeding-care questions with low-friction either/or formats. The visual demonstration gives practical context while the answer takes only seconds.
  5. 5. Ρωτήστε μας πού θα το βρείτε / Ask where to find it
    Ελληνικά: «Μας ρωτάτε συχνά πού θα βρείτε GRUBZ. Πείτε μας μόνο την περιοχή σας σε μήνυμα και θα προσπαθήσουμε να βοηθήσουμε με τη διαθεσιμότητα. Στο μεταξύ: ποια είναι η μία λιχουδιά που κρατάτε πάντα για μια ξεχωριστή στιγμή με τις ενήλικες κότες σας;»
    English: “You often ask where to find GRUBZ. Send us just your area in a message and we will try to help with availability. Meanwhile: what is the one treat you save for a special moment with your adult hens?”
    Suggested format: Warm product-and-flock photo; availability CTA only in the final line.
    Engagement hook: A public care question with availability handled privately.
    Why it works: Local availability is a recurring marketplace need in the historical pattern library. Keeping the practical request private and leading publicly with a shared ritual makes the post useful rather than sales-heavy.
  6. 6. Το σήμα της λιχουδιάς / The treat-time cue
    Ελληνικά: «Με ήχο: το απλό σήμα που σημαίνει “ώρα για λίγη εξερεύνηση”. Χωρίς μουσική και χωρίς σενάριο—μόνο η καθημερινή αντίδραση των ενηλίκων κοτών μας όταν έρχονται λίγα GRUBZ. Ποιο σήμα αναγνωρίζει αμέσως η δική σας παρέα;»
    English: “Sound on: the simple cue that means ‘a little exploration time.’ No music and no script—just our adult hens’ everyday reaction when a few GRUBZ arrive. What cue does your flock recognise straight away?”
    Suggested format: Five-to-seven-second sound-on vertical video at flock level.
    Engagement hook: Invite a short sound, word, or routine answer.
    Why it works: Short reaction videos and familiar flock behaviour were strong visual patterns in the historical sample. Real-time proof creates an emotional moment without relying on a hard sell.

    Ελληνικά: «Για όσους ρωτούν πού θα βρουν GRUBZ: είναι μια λιχουδιά εντόμων για ενήλικες κότες, σε μικρή ποσότητα και ως μέρος μιας ισορροπημένης καθημερινής φροντίδας. Στείλτε μας μήνυμα για πληροφορίες διαθεσιμότητας.»
    English: “For anyone asking where to find GRUBZ: it is an insect treat for adult hens, offered in small amounts as part of balanced everyday care. Message us for availability details.”
    Suggested format: Clean product-and-flock photo with an availability card in the first comment.
    Engagement hook: Invite a direct message rather than price negotiation in the thread.
    Why it works: Marketplace groups respond to clear availability cues, while the care-first framing keeps the post helpful rather than pushy.
  6. 6. Πριν ακουστεί ο κουβάς / Before the bucket lands
    Ελληνικά: «Βάλτε ήχο: το κοπάδι ακούει τον κουβά πριν καν φτάσουμε στο κοτέτσι. Μια μικρή απογευματινή στιγμή με GRUBZ και πολλή περιέργεια.»
    English: “Sound on: the flock hears the bucket before we even reach the coop. A small afternoon GRUBZ moment and a lot of curiosity.”
    Suggested format: 6–8 second sound-on reel; no music needed.
    Engagement hook: “Do your hens recognise a sound before they see you?”
    Why it works: Short sensory proof and an everyday flock reaction are more natural in busy feeds than a product explanation.
